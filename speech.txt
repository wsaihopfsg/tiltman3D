Good morning, everyone! It’s great to see all of you. For Keith and Zin sitting so far back, yes, I am actually standing up right now.

I’ve decided to use my time today to talk about something that has literally shaped my entire perspective on life—mostly because my perspective is fixed at an altitude of about chest-height to the rest of the world.

Let's start from the very beginning. My parents named me Sai Ho. Now, for those of you who speak <b>Cantonese</b>, you already know the punchline. "Sai Ho" sounds exactly like "Small Size." Self-fulfilling prophecy, huh? When I was in kindergarten, I made my parents call me Dai Ho, but the curse was not broken.

Being this height comes with a very unique set of daily challenges. Supermarket runs are basically extreme sports. If the Lee Kum Kee premium soy sauce is on the top shelf at NTUC, I just have to accept that my family is eating the house brand that week—unless I want to use my qinggong to stand on the lowest shelf, which often does not help. Once, an NTUC staff member told me, "Next time, ask for help." Then there are the physical misunderstandings. Because of my frame, the zi char stall owner sometimes mistakes me for a female and calls me xiaomei. It has happened more times than I can count.

You’d think the dating world would be rough. The conventional wisdom is that I am technically "too short to date." But I consider myself an overachiever. I ended up marrying a wonderful woman who is actually taller than me. When we walk together, I just assume everyone thinks she’s a very generous celebrity doing charity work.

But my true character was forged in the Singapore Armed Forces. When it was time for my pre-enlistment medical check-up, they measured me, and I had a devastating realization: I was exactly 7 centimeters too tall to be fully exempted from National Service. Seven centimeters! A slightly thick pair of socks ruined my chance at early civilian freedom.

Somehow, I made it into Officer Cadet School. I remember when I was at Brunei airport and met some Aussie soldiers. They were—OMG—three times my size! I also have fond memories of using the 84mm recoilless rifle. When I adopted the firing position, my CO exclaimed, "The 84 is as long as you!" I powered through, thanks to the help of my platoon mates and even my officers, and eventually made it to my Commissioning Parade. I went to the logistics store to loan my ceremonial No. 1 uniform. We searched the entire inventory. Not a single uniform fit my height. Luckily, the uniform is worn tucked-out, and I just made a fold to shorten my pants. Hence, I officially cemented my legacy as the shortest SAF officer to ever march on the parade square.

I always excuse myself from sword drill. My scabbard is almost dragging on the floor! Should have asked for a kid-sized sword

The most humbling experience, however, happened outside of uniform. I was having a night out at Hooters over at Clarke Quay. I went to the restroom, and a massive Caucasian guy stepped up to the urinal right next to mine. He looked down at me, looked back at the wall, and did this (insert pinching gesture). No words were exchanged. None were needed. I smiled and exited quickly. But AMDK was real.

But it’s not all bad! Being vertically challenged has its perks. For one, I still get ID checked when I order a drink. Do you have any idea how incredible it feels to be a 49-year-old father of two and have a bouncer at a bar look at you suspiciously to make sure you're legally allowed to hold a pint? I hand over my IC with absolute pride.

And let me add a few other minor things you tall people take for granted. Buying pants? I don't buy pants; I buy fabric that I immediately pay a tailor to chop in half. When I visit the outlet malls, even the kids' sizes seem too big sometimes. I have never once had my knees touch the seat in front of me on an economy flight. And in group photos, I am always right in front, surrounded by ladies.

Lastly, I want to share with you a book chapter that I wrote on Interference Mitigation in LTE. Yes, it's the shortest chapter in the whole book. No surprise there.

Thank you! And I am HK Wong, from Hong Kong!
