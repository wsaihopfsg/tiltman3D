Good morning, everyone! It’s great to see all of you. 

I’ve decided to use my time today to talk about a disability that none of you ever has to deal with. That is, for being very short. Though Pooi Looi is not much taller, but smaller-sized females are often described "petite" or "cute". Short male species are just, SHORT. For Keith and Zin sitting so far back, yes, I am actually standing up right now. 

Let's start from the very beginning. My parents named me Sai Ho. Now, for those of you who speak Cantonese, you already know the punchline. "Sai Ho" <i>sounds exactly like</i> "Small Size." I was always shorter than everyone else. When I was in kindergarten, I made my parents call me Dai Ho, where Dai is the exact opposite of Sai. You can't blame me, look at Kah Loong, he is so LONG! 

Being this height comes with a very unique set of daily challenges. Supermarket runs are basically extreme sports. If the Lee Kum Kee premium soy sauce is on the top shelf at NTUC, I just have to accept that my family is eating the house brand that week—unless I want to use my qinggong to <i>stand on the lowest shelf</i>, which often does not help. Once, an NTUC <i>staff told me</i>, "Uncle, next time, ask for help." Then there are the physical misunderstandings. Because of my frame, the cai png stall owner sometimes mistakes me for a female and calls me xiaojie. 

You’d think the dating world would be rough. The conventional wisdom is that I am technically "too short to date." But I consider myself an overachiever. I ended up marrying a wonderful woman who is actually taller than me. When <i>we walk together</i>, I just assume everyone thinks she’s a very generous celebrity doing charity work. 

But my true character was forged in the Singapore Armed Forces. When it was time for my pre-enlistment medical check-up, they measured me, and I had a devastating realization: I was exactly 7 centimeters too tall to be fully exempted from National Service. Seven centimeters! Should have worn a thinner pair of socks that day and earn my civilian freedom earlier.

Somehow, I made it into Officer Cadet School. In fact Kenneth was my OCS senior, but he is normal - normal height, normal looks, normal intelligence. I faced many more challenges that as a shorter than usual person. The <i>low wall</i> is actually very high for me! I was at Brunei airport for our jungle training, I saw some Aussie soldiers. They were—OMG—three times my size! It was lucky that we were not wearing camouflage uniforms at the airport, otherwise I could have been mistaken as a child soldier.

I also have fond memories of using the 84mm recoilless rifle. When I adopted the <i>firing position</i>, my CO exclaimed, "The 84 is as long as you!" I powered through, thanks to the help of my platoon mates and even my officers, and eventually made it to my Commissioning Parade. I went to the logistics store to loan my ceremonial No. 1 uniform. We searched the entire inventory. Not a single uniform fit my height. Luckily, the uniform is worn tucked-out, and I just made a fold to shorten my pants. Hence, I officially cemented my legacy as the shortest SAF officer to ever march on the parade square.

I always <i>excuse myself from sword drill</i>. My scabbard is almost dragging on the floor! Should have asked for a kid-sized sword.

The most humbling experience, however, happened outside of uniform. I was having a night-out at Hooters over at Clarke Quay. I went to the restroom, and a Caucasian guy stepped up to the urinal right next to mine. He peeped as I was peeing, and <i>did this gesture</i>. I smiled in shame and exited quickly. But ang moh dua kee was real.

But it’s not all bad! Being vertically challenged has its perks. For one, I still get ID checked when I order a drink sometimes. Do you have any idea how incredible it feels to be a 49-year-old father of two and have a bouncer at a bar to be asked for ID to make sure I was legal to drink? 

And let me add a few other minor things you tall people take for granted. Buying pants? I don't buy pants; I buy fabric that I immediately pay a tailor to chop in half. When I visit the outlet malls, even the kids' sizes seem too big sometimes. I have never once had my knees touch the seat in front of me on an economy flight. And in group photos, I am always right in front, surrounded by ladies.

Lastly, I want to share with you a book chapter that I wrote on Interference Mitigation in LTE. Yes, it's the shortest chapter in the whole book. No surprise there.

Thank you! I am HK Wong, from Hong Kong!
